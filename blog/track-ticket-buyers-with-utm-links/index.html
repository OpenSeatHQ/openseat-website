How to Track Where Your Ticket Buyers Come From with UTM Links

Oct 29, 2026

Why your checkout link needs a UTM tag

If you post the same plain checkout link everywhere, you can see that tickets sold, but not which post, story, email or flyer sold them. A UTM link fixes that: it's your normal OpenSeat checkout link with a few short tags added to the end, and those tags tell your analytics exactly where each visitor came from.

With UTM links you can answer questions like:

  • Did my Instagram Reel or my Facebook event drive more checkout visits?
  • Was the newsletter worth sending?
  • Did the flyer QR code at the coffee shop get scanned at all?

Knowing this tells you where to spend your limited promo time on the next event.

Example: an Instagram post

Say you're promoting your Fall Oktoberfest on Instagram.

Without a UTM tag, you'd paste your regular checkout link:

https://openseat.cc/e/fall-oktoberfest

Visitors from that post blend in with everyone else. Your analytics might group them under "instagram.com" or even "direct" (mobile apps often strip where a click came from), so you can't tell this post apart from your bio link or a friend's share.

With a UTM tag, you'd paste this instead:

https://openseat.cc/e/fall-oktoberfest?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=fall_oktoberfest_2026&utm_content=lineup_reel

It still opens the exact same checkout page. But now your analytics can show you exactly how many people arrived from the lineup Reel, separate from every other link you've shared.

Use your real event link in place of the example openseat.cc/e/fall-oktoberfest link throughout this article.

Before you start

You'll need two things:

  • Your event's checkout link. Copy it from your OpenSeat event page.
  • A list of where you're promoting. Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, your email list, partner shares, posters. Each place gets its own UTM link.

The tool you'll use is Google's free Campaign URL Builder. No account or login is needed to build links.

How to build a UTM link

  1. Open the builder. Go to ga-dev-tools.google/ga4/campaign-url-builder and stay on the Web tab.
  2. Paste your checkout link into Website URL. Example: https://openseat.cc/e/fall-oktoberfest
  3. Fill in Campaign source (required). This is where the link lives. Example: instagram
  4. Fill in Campaign medium. This is the type of channel. Example: social
  5. Fill in Campaign name. This is the event or promo push. Example: fall_oktoberfest_2026
  6. Optional: fill in Campaign content. Use this to tell apart two links in the same place. Example: lineup_reel vs bio_link
  7. Skip the rest. Campaign ID and Campaign term are mostly for paid ads. Leave them blank.
  8. Copy your link. The finished link appears at the bottom of the page as you type. Click Copy URL and paste it into your post.

Repeat for each place you promote. It takes about 30 seconds per link once you have the hang of it.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Stick to lowercase. UTM values are case-sensitive, so Instagram and instagram show up as two different sources.
  • Use underscores, not spaces. fall_oktoberfest_2026 reads cleanly; spaces turn into %20 in the link.
  • Pick names once and reuse them. Keep a simple note or spreadsheet of the links you've made so you spell things the same way every time.
  • Never use UTM links on your own website. Tagging links between your own pages overwrites where the visitor originally came from.
  • Shorten long links if you like. A link shortener is fine for printed materials or captions, as long as you shorten the full UTM link, not the plain one.
  • Test before you post. Paste the finished link into your browser and confirm it opens your checkout page.

Where to see your results

If your event page is connected to Google Analytics 4:

  1. Open Google Analytics and go to Reports > Acquisition > Traffic acquisition.
  2. Change the table's main column to Session source / medium to compare channels, such as instagram / social vs newsletter / email.
  3. Switch it to Session campaign to see totals for each event.
  4. To compare individual posts, add Session manual ad content as a secondary column. That's where your utm_content values show up.

Check back a few days after posting. Compare the channels at the end of each event to see where your audience actually comes from, then put more effort there next time.

FAQ

Will a UTM link change what buyers see?

No. It opens the same checkout page. The tags just ride along in the address bar.

Do I need a new link for every post?

You need a new link for every place you want to measure separately. If you only care about Instagram as a whole, one Instagram link is enough. If you want to compare a Reel to a Story, give each its own utm_content.

Can I reuse links for my next event?

Make new ones with a new campaign name, such as spring_market_2027. That keeps each event's results separate.

Why do some visits still show as "direct"?

Some apps and copied links drop referral info. That's exactly why UTM tags help: visits from a tagged link are credited correctly even when the app hides where the click came from.
